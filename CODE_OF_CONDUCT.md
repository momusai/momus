# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Argue about the work, not the person doing it.

This project attracts security researchers, and security culture has a habit of
mistaking contempt for rigour. It isn't. You can be exacting about a detector
that false-positives without being unpleasant to whoever wrote it.

## What we expect

- **Critique the code, the attack, or the claim.** "This detector matches a
  refusal that quotes the canary" is useful. "Did you even test this?" is not.
- **Assume the other person is competent and acting in good faith** until you
  have real evidence otherwise. Most disagreements here are about context
  someone lacks, not ability.
- **Be patient with newcomers.** Writing an attack that can't cry wolf is
  genuinely hard and the failure modes are unintuitive. Explaining why a
  detector is unsafe is part of the work, not an interruption to it.
- **Accept being wrong gracefully.** Everyone who has worked on this pack has
  shipped a detector that turned out to be unsafe. That's the process.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks, in issues, PRs, or anywhere else
  the project's name is on it.
- Demeaning anyone for who they are — including, but not limited to, their
  background, nationality, gender, sexuality, disability, age, religion, or
  level of experience.
- Sexualised language or imagery, or unwelcome sexual attention.
- Publishing someone's private information without their permission.
- Sustained disruption: reopening settled arguments to wear people down, or
  derailing threads to make a point.

## Scope

This applies in the repository — issues, pull requests, discussions, commit
messages — and anywhere someone is representing the project publicly.

## Reporting

Email the maintainers, or use GitHub's private reporting on the Security tab if
you would rather not send email. Reports are handled privately. Tell us what
happened, where, and what you'd like to see done; we'll acknowledge within a few
days.

Maintainers may warn, remove content, or ban someone from the project, depending
on what happened. We will not pretend a serious incident was a misunderstanding,
and we will not treat a first minor lapse as a capital offence.

If a maintainer is the problem, report it to a different maintainer. Maintainers
recuse themselves from decisions about their own conduct.

## A note specific to this project

Momus is a tool for attacking systems. Discussing attack techniques, jailbreak
prompts, and exploitation in detail is the point and is always in scope. Using
the project to organise or assist an attack on a system you don't have
permission to test is not — see [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).
